National Insurance (NI) is a tax on earnings. It builds your entitlement to the **State Pension** and some benefits, such as contribution-based Jobseeker's Allowance and Maternity Allowance. If you're an employee, you pay **Class 1** NI through payroll, and your employer pays its own NI on top.

## Employee NI rates 2026/27
| Annual earnings | Rate | Monthly threshold (HMRC payroll) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to £12,570 (Primary Threshold) | 0% | up to £1,048 |
| £12,570 – £50,270 | 8% | £1,048 – £4,189 |
| Over £50,270 (Upper Earnings Limit) | 2% | over £4,189 |

The thresholds are the same everywhere in the UK, including Scotland. Unlike Income Tax, NI is identical in all four nations.

## Worked examples
| Salary | Calculation | Employee NI a year | Monthly average |
|---|---|---|---|
| £25,000 | (£25,000 − £12,570) × 8% | £994.40 | £82.87 |
| £40,000 | (£40,000 − £12,570) × 8% | £2,194.40 | £182.87 |
| £60,000 | £37,700 × 8% + (£60,000 − £50,270) × 2% | £3,210.60 | £267.55 |

Above £50,270 you still pay NI, but only at 2%. That's why the marginal rate for a higher-rate taxpayer is **42%** (40% tax + 2% NI), not 48%.

## Employer National Insurance
Employers pay **15% on earnings above £5,000** a year (the Secondary Threshold, £417 a month). This doesn't come out of your pay, but it's part of what you cost to employ:

| Salary | Employer NI | Total cost to employer (before pension) |
|---|---|---|
| £25,000 | £3,000 | £28,000 |
| £40,000 | £5,250 | £45,250 |
| £60,000 | £8,250 | £68,250 |

Many small employers can claim the **Employment Allowance**, which reduces their employer NI bill each year. Some salary sacrifice schemes exist partly because they save the employer this 15% too.

## NI is calculated per pay period, not per year
Income Tax is worked out on a cumulative basis across the tax year, but NI is calculated **separately for each pay period**. A bonus month can therefore cost more NI than the same money spread over the year.

*Example:* you normally earn £3,000 a month and pay £156.16 NI. In a month where you're paid £8,000 including a bonus, you pay 8% on £1,048 to £4,189 (£251.28) plus 2% on the rest (£76.22): **£327.50**. There's no refund at year end, even if your annual pay is under £50,270. Company directors are the exception: they're assessed on an annual basis. Try your own figures, including a bonus month, in the [National Insurance calculator](/national-insurance-calculator).

## What your NI pays for
- **The new State Pension:** you need **35 qualifying years** for the full amount and at least 10 years to get anything. Earning above the Lower Earnings Limit counts as a qualifying year, even before you start paying NI.
- **Credits:** if you're not working, you may get NI credits automatically, for example while claiming Child Benefit for a child under 12, Carer's Allowance or Universal Credit.
- **Gaps:** you can usually pay voluntary Class 3 contributions to fill gaps in your record. Check your record first at [GOV.UK – Check your National Insurance record](https://www.gov.uk/check-national-insurance-record).

## How to pay less NI legally
- **Salary sacrifice:** pension, cycle-to-work and electric car schemes reduce your pay before NI is worked out, saving 8% (or 2% above £50,270). From **April 2029**, the NI saving on pension salary sacrifice will be capped at £2,000 a year of contributions. See our [salary sacrifice guide](/guides/salary-sacrifice-pension).
- **Net pay and relief-at-source pensions don't reduce NI.** Only salary sacrifice does.
- **Reaching State Pension age:** employees stop paying employee NI, although employers still pay theirs.

## Self-employed?
Self-employed people pay **Class 4 NI**: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above. It's paid through Self Assessment rather than payroll. Work out your bill with the [self-employed tax calculator](/self-employed-tax-calculator).

## Frequently asked questions
**Do I pay NI on my pension income?** No. NI isn't charged on pension income, or on earnings after State Pension age.

**Does Scotland have different NI rates?** No. NI is UK-wide. Only Income Tax differs in Scotland.

**Why did my NI go up in one month?** Usually because of a bonus, overtime or backpay. NI is calculated on each pay period separately (see above).

**Does a student loan affect NI?** No. Student loan repayments are calculated separately and don't change your NI.
