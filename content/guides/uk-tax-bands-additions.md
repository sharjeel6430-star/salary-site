## Your tax rate vs your marginal rate
Moving into the 40% band doesn't mean all your income is taxed at 40%. Only the pounds above £50,270 are. On £60,000 you pay 40% on £9,730, but your *overall* (effective) Income Tax rate is 19%. Your **marginal rate** is what matters for pay rises, overtime and bonuses, and it's highest between £100,000 and £125,140, at 62% including NI.

## Fiscal drag: why you pay more tax every year
The Personal Allowance and the higher-rate threshold are **frozen until April 2031**. As wages rise, more of your income falls into higher bands. A £50,000 salary that rises 4% a year will cross into the 40% band next year, even though the rules haven't changed. ## Frequently asked questions
**What tax band am I in on £50,000?** The basic rate (20%). £50,000 is £270 below the £50,270 higher-rate threshold. A Scottish taxpayer on £50,000 is in the 42% higher band, which starts at £43,663.

**Are tax brackets based on gross or taxable income?** In GOV.UK tables, the bands apply to taxable income after the Personal Allowance. The figures above (£12,571–£50,270 etc.) are the equivalent *gross* salary ranges for someone with the standard £12,570 allowance.
