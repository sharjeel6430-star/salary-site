Student loan repayments are taken through payroll once your income goes over your plan's threshold. You repay a percentage of the amount **above** the threshold, not of your whole salary, and nothing at all if you earn less than it.

## Thresholds 2026/27
| Plan | Annual threshold | Monthly | Rate | Written off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan 1 | £26,900 | £2,241 | 9% | 25 years after first due to repay (loans from Sept 2006) |
| Plan 2 | £29,385 | £2,448 | 9% | 30 years after the April you were first due to repay |
| Plan 4 (Scotland) | £33,795 | £2,816 | 9% | 30 years, or age 65 for older loans |
| Plan 5 | £25,000 | £2,083 | 9% | **40 years** after the April you were first due to repay |

*Example:* on £35,000 with a Plan 2 loan you repay 9% × (£35,000 − £29,385) = **£505.35 a year**, about £42 a month.

## Which plan am I on?
- **Plan 1:** started in England or Wales before September 2012, or from Northern Ireland.
- **Plan 2:** started in England or Wales between September 2012 and July 2023.
- **Plan 4:** student from Scotland.
- **Plan 5:** started an undergraduate course in England on or after 1 August 2023.
- **Postgraduate Loan:** master's or doctoral loan from England or Wales.

Not sure? Log in to your [Student Loans Company account](https://www.gov.uk/sign-in-to-manage-your-student-loan-balance) or check the plan type on your payslip.

## If you have more than one loan
- **Plan 1 and Plan 2:** you repay 9% of income over the *lower* (Plan 1) threshold. The money is split between the two loans.
- **Undergraduate plus Postgraduate:** you repay **both** at once: 9% over your undergraduate threshold *and* 6% over £21,000. That's 15% of income above both thresholds, on top of tax and NI.

## Plan 5: why it costs more
Plan 5 has the **lowest threshold (£25,000)** and the **longest term (40 years)**, so most Plan 5 graduates will repay for most of their working life. On a £40,000 salary, a Plan 5 graduate repays £395 a year more than a Plan 2 graduate on the same pay.

## Should you overpay?
For most Plan 2 and Plan 5 graduates, **probably not**. Most won't repay the full balance before it's written off, so an overpayment only reduces a debt that would have been cancelled anyway. Overpaying makes most sense if you expect to earn a high salary for most of your career and clear the loan in full regardless. Plan 1 balances are smaller, so overpaying is worth considering if you're close to clearing yours. Money in a pension or ISA usually works harder.

## Self-employed or paid irregularly?
If you're self-employed, you repay through Self Assessment, based on your annual income. If you're employed, repayments are calculated on each pay period. A bonus month can trigger a repayment even if your annual salary is below the threshold. You can claim a refund from the SLC after the tax year ends.

## Frequently asked questions
**Does a student loan affect my credit score or mortgage?** It doesn't appear on your credit file, but lenders count the monthly repayment as an outgoing when they work out affordability.

**Does salary sacrifice reduce student loan repayments?** Yes. Repayments are worked out on your pay after salary sacrifice, so you save 9% on top of tax and NI.

**When do thresholds change?** They're usually reviewed each April. Plan 1 and Plan 4 thresholds normally rise with inflation; Plan 2 and Plan 5 are set by government decision. The Postgraduate threshold has stayed at £21,000 since 2016.
